# Evidence beyond ESCAP's database

ESCAP's completed answers are the answer key we grade against. They are not the ceiling.

Where this system scores a cell higher than they did, one of two things is true: we are wrong, or
we found something they did not. The difference is decidable, and this file is where the decidable
version is written down — a cell, the score on each side, the instrument they did not cite, and the
words in it, verified to be there.

The standing rule this serves is that we never fit the output to the answer key. A disagreement is
kept and defended, or it is fixed because it is wrong. It is never quietly reconciled.

The gate prints these as it finds them, under **Beyond ESCAP**, so this file is a record rather
than a source of truth. What the gate cannot do is tell a finding from a mistake — that judgement
is made here, once, in writing.

## Australia 6.2 — local storage requirement

**Ours 1 · theirs 0.5.** ESCAP cites the My Health Records Act 2012.

The band is theirs: *"Local storage requirement for all sectors or personal data, **or more than
one measure in category (2)**"*. We found three measures in that middle category where they
counted one, and their own rule escalates on the third.

| Instrument | Provision | The words |
|---|---|---|
| Data Availability and Transparency Act 2022 | s 77B, conditions of accreditation | "prohibit the entity from storing or accessing, or providing access to, scheme data outside Australia" |
| Corporations Act 2001 | s 289, place where financial records are kept | "must be kept in this jurisdiction" |
| Corporations Act 2001 | s 289A, place where sustainability records are kept | "sufficient written information about those matters must be kept in this jurisdiction" |

The two company-records provisions are ordinary corporate law rather than data policy, which is
the likeliest reason a researcher working from the data-protection literature would not reach
them. They are still local storage requirements on their face, and the indicator counts measures,
not subject areas.

**Status:** kept. Reviewable in one click from the citations above.

## Australia 7.2 — dedicated cybersecurity framework

**Ours 0 (the best band) · theirs 0.5.** ESCAP cites the Security of Critical Infrastructure Act
2018, which is sectoral, and scored the middle band accordingly.

Australia has since enacted the **Cyber Security Act 2024**, a dedicated horizontal framework. Our
reading records it as establishing a framework, applying across sectors, and dedicated to the
subject — the three facts that band turns on.

This is a currency difference, not a disagreement about the law. Their answer was right when it
was written.

**Status:** kept. Worth flagging to ESCAP as an update to their own row rather than as a dispute.
